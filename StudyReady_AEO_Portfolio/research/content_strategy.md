# AEO content strategy

## Editorial promise

Clear study guidance a learner can try today, with the reasoning and sources visible. The purpose is to teach, not to publish a large volume of question-shaped pages.

## Page model

Each lesson should:

1. Address one learner task or tightly related question cluster.
2. Use a descriptive title and one visible H1.
3. Give a short, scope-aware answer near the top.
4. Explain why the action may help, how to try it, and when another approach is needed.
5. Include a worked example, check, practice task, or useful download where appropriate.
6. State the intended learner stage and any curriculum boundary.
7. Identify the real author and qualified reviewer, show relevant credentials without exaggeration, and cite sources.
8. Link to prerequisite, related, and next-step lessons using descriptive text.
9. Include a review date only after a meaningful review and a route for corrections.
10. Keep the core answer available as accessible HTML text.

## Initial topic clusters

- Active recall and self-testing
- Spaced practice and revision schedules
- Flashcards and memory prompts
- Revision planning and workload
- Practice questions and error review
- Note-making and understanding
- Parent and educator support

The question bank is a seed set. Prioritize topics after interviews, SERP review, and the real site's Search Console data. Do not attach search-volume or difficulty estimates without data and date/source.

## Trust and accessibility standard

Review educational claims against primary research or established education guidance. Explain the audience and limits of evidence in plain language. Use semantic headings, descriptive links, keyboard-operable controls, readable contrast, and equivalent text for meaningful visuals. Have a qualified educator review these prototype drafts and verify all links before publication.
