How to learn from practice test mistakes

StudyReady Editorial Team · Draft prepared 22 November 2026 · General secondary-school study support

Reviewing a mistake works best when you identify why it happened and try a corrected problem.

A practice test is useful when it changes what you do next. Identify the kind of error, check the correct reasoning, and try a similar task after you understand the correction.

What kind of mistake did I make?

Was a fact missing? Did you misunderstand the question, choose the wrong method, make a calculation slip, or run out of time? Naming the error helps you choose a response.

How do I correct it?

Return to a trustworthy explanation or ask your teacher. Write the correct steps in your own words. Close the solution and try the question again.

How do I check that I learned from it?

Try a related but not identical question later. Explain why the method fits and complete the new task.

What should I record?

Keep a short error log: topic, error type, correction, and next action. Do not treat one practice score as a prediction of a final grade.

Common questions

Should I redo it straight away?

First understand the correction. Then redo it without looking and later try a similar question.

Does one low practice score mean I will fail?

A practice result is one piece of information about preparation under particular conditions. Use errors to guide study and ask a teacher for help.
