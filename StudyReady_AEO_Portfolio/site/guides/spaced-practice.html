Spaced practice: how to spread out your revision

StudyReady Editorial Team · Draft prepared 22 November 2026 · General secondary-school study support

Spaced practice means returning to learning over separate sessions instead of cramming it all at once.

Spaced practice means revisiting material across multiple sessions with time between them. It can help you remember learning for longer than one concentrated session, especially when you try to retrieve the material before checking it. There is no single perfect schedule for everyone.

How do I make a simple plan?

List the topics you need to learn. Put a short review on your calendar on more than one day. Begin with a manageable gap, then adjust: revisit sooner when recall is difficult and leave longer when you can explain the topic accurately.

What should I do in each review?

Start by recalling the topic from memory, then check your notes or answer key. Work a fresh problem for a skill. Record mistakes and uncertain points for the next review.

Is there a perfect number of days between sessions?

No. The useful interval depends on the deadline, difficulty, how well you can retrieve it, and your available time. Treat a schedule as an adaptable plan.

What if an exam is soon?

Prioritize important topics and mix short reviews with practice questions. Avoid spending all your time making a timetable.

Common questions

Is spaced practice just studying less?

No. It changes when you return to the material; each session still needs focused work and feedback.

Should I space every topic equally?

No. Give more attention to topics that remain difficult while returning to all important material over time.
