How to make a realistic revision plan

StudyReady Editorial Team · Draft prepared 22 November 2026 · General secondary-school study support

A useful revision plan turns a list of topics into short sessions you can actually complete.

A revision plan is a working schedule, not a promise to study every hour. Start with the topics and deadlines you have, choose small tasks, and leave room to adjust.

1. List topics and deadlines

Write each subject, the topics you need to cover, and the dates that matter. Check the list against class materials.

2. Choose a useful task

Replace “study biology” with an action: explain a process from memory, solve three problems, or review errors. Give the task a clear finish point.

3. Prioritize and schedule

Give earlier attention to important, unfamiliar, or difficult topics. Spread topics across days when possible. Keep sessions realistic for school, home, and rest.

4. Review the plan

Note what you could do and what needs another attempt. Move unfinished tasks rather than filling every remaining hour. Ask a teacher for help prioritizing if needed.

Common questions

How long should a session be?

Choose a length that fits your attention, task, and schedule. Try a short block and adjust based on how well you work.

Should I include breaks?

Yes. Include breaks and normal rest in a schedule you can sustain.
